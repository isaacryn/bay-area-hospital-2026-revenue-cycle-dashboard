This dashboard is built on three public datasets, joined and validated step by step rather than merged blindly. Each step below is a real decision made against the actual data, not a template pipeline.

  1. 01

    Three source files, three formats

    CA HCAI Q1 2026 Hospital Quarterly Financial & Utilization Report, the HCAI 2025 Annual Utilization Report, and CA Dept. of Finance county population estimates — each with different column names, header structures, and shapes.

  2. 02

    Caught three data-quality bugs before merging

    Multi-word county names split across two physical rows in the population file, one county's year field stored as text instead of a number, and leftover whitespace from stitching names back together — each verified with before/after checks, not assumed fixed.

  3. 03

    Validated join keys first

    Confirmed facility numbers matched exactly between the two HCAI files, and county names matched the population file, before running any merge — a merge on an unverified key silently drops or duplicates rows with no error thrown.

  4. 04

    Scoped to the Bay Area, not all of "Northern California"

    Checked first: a wider Northern CA region would include 9 counties represented by a single hospital each — turning a "county comparison" into one facility's numbers wearing a county label. The Bay Area (9 counties, all multi-hospital) keeps every comparison statistically real.

  5. 05

    Applied a volume floor

    Excluded 3 facilities under 50 discharges/quarter from ratio-based comparisons — a 5-discharge pediatric specialty hospital produced a $940K "revenue per discharge" figure that reflected sample size, not performance.

  6. 06

    Aggregated counties by summing dollars first, not averaging ratios

    County-level margin is computed from summed revenue and summed expense, then divided — not by averaging each hospital's individual margin. Averaging ratios directly would let a small hospital's percentage count as much as a large one's, misrepresenting the county.

  7. 07

    Flagged statistical outliers

    Napa County sits at −2.34 standard deviations from the Bay Area mean margin — driven by Napa State Hospital's non-standard state-funded structure, not a data error. Explained in context rather than just labeled an outlier.

  8. 08

    Exported a lean, purpose-built dataset

    72 facilities, 9 counties, only the fields this dashboard actually visualizes — not the full 156-column working table.
